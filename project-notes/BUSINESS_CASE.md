# Business case

Your prototype shows *what* the product looks like. This document explains *why* it could become a business. Answer in your own words and refer to course concepts by name. See the term project brief on Moodle for length and grading.

## 1. The idea

- **Customer:** Students and job seekers preparing for applications and interviews across careers such as investment banking, consulting, accounting, engineering, computer science, and software engineering.
- **Problem:** They need to organize preparation around their career goals and available time, practice for different interview rounds, and find relevant student career pages. The prompts did not say how they currently solve this problem.
- **Solution:** UpNext provides a personalized “This Week” preparation roadmap, adjusts preparation priorities to the time before an interview or application opening, and includes interview questions and “What came up?” topics that change by interview round. The dashboard also links to company student/early-career pages, with logos, in a scrollable row.
- **Revenue:** Not discussed in the chat.

## 2. Where IT creates value

The prototype brings career-preparation steps and interview practice into one browser-based tool and provides links to company career pages. Personalizing the roadmap by role, timing, and interview round is a form of differentiation. The chat did not establish a sales or distribution model, and the current prototype does not demonstrate switching costs. User-provided career and preparation information is used to tailor the experience; the prototype saves progress in the browser.

## 3. Network effects

The prototype does not currently demonstrate a network effect: its preparation tools and company career links can be used by one person without other users.

- Is it a **one-sided** or **two-sided** market? Who are the sides? As shown, it is a one-sided tool for students and job seekers; there is no employer-facing side in the prototype.
- Where does the extra value come from as the network grows? No additional network value from user growth is established in the chat.
- How would you solve the "empty network" problem at launch? The roadmap and interview practice are usable by an individual, and the company career links do not depend on other users joining.
- Point to the part of your prototype that shows this. The personalized “This Week” roadmap, Topic Guide, “What came up?” topics, and company-career link row.

## 4. Can it be copied? (the moat question)

Anyone in this class could rebuild your website in a weekend with the same AI tool. So the code is **not** your competitive advantage.

- What about your business is **valuable, rare, hard to imitate and well-organized** (VRIN)? The personalized preparation flow may be valuable to users, but no rare or hard-to-imitate advantage was established in the chat.
- What would a competitor with a bigger budget need to copy you, and how long would it take? The chat does not estimate this. The current visible features are a roadmap, round-specific interview content, and links to company student-career pages.
- Which of your advantages get *stronger* over time (data, network, brand, switching costs)? None was established in the chat; the prototype does not demonstrate network effects or switching costs.

## 5. Cloud and operations

The current prototype is browser-based and saves progress locally; the chat did not describe a backend or cloud-hosted database. Company logos are loaded from an external favicon service, so logo display depends on that service. The latest opportunity section links to company career pages instead of claiming to verify live openings. A real operating model, cloud services, and their costs were not discussed in the chat.

## 6. Risks and ethics

The preparation information is saved in the browser, so it is not shown in the chat as being backed up or synchronized across devices. Company career pages and externally loaded logos may change or become unavailable. Earlier live-listing designs also showed that job feeds may not provide direct employer application links or complete coverage; the latest design uses company career pages instead. The chat did not define a broader risk-management plan.
